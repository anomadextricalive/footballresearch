[ 01 ] SITUATION

Palace is not for sale as a club. The last transaction was a stake transfer, not a takeover: in July 2025, Woody Johnson (NY Jets owner) completed purchase of John Textor/Eagle Football's 43% shareholding for £190M, implying a whole-club value north of £400M. Steve Parish, Josh Harris and David Blitzer stayed in as partners. There is no indication any of the four current owners intend to sell control.

The Textor exit wasn't opportunistic — it was forced. Eagle Football also controlled Lyon, breaching UEFA's multi-club ownership rule. That conflict cost Palace their spot in the 2025-26 Europa League (won via FA Cup, their first major trophy) — they were demoted to the Conference League instead. Selling the stake was the compliance fix.

Read-through: the only realistic entry point today is a future minority stake, either if Harris/Blitzer trim further or Parish eventually monetizes his holding. There is no distressed-asset or forced-sale angle here — this is a stable, well-run club with an engaged ownership bloc.

[ 06 ] BOTTOM LINE

Pass on primary acquisition — there's nothing to acquire. Palace has no forced seller, no distressed pricing, and no path to control at any cheque size a private buyer would write today. This is the opposite profile of the desk's Wolves/Preston-style plays: fairly valued, professionally run, ownership-locked.

If capital must go into Palace specifically, the only sane structure is a passive minority position should Harris, Blitzer, or Parish ever trim further — priced off the £400M Johnson mark, not the £598M Forbes mark. Otherwise, redeploy toward the EFL-tier distressed and asymmetric plays already flagged in the wider dataset.
